# OIO Social Voice Guide

## Purpose

This guide exists to help draft social posts that sound like **OIO**, not like a generic brand, agency, or influencer account.

The goal is not polished marketing.
The goal is to sound like a real grassroots motorsports team that lives this stuff.

---

## Core Voice

OIO sounds like:

- a knowledgeable car person
- a racer or builder talking to other enthusiasts
- someone amused by the chaos
- someone who respects the machines but does not talk like a museum curator
- someone telling a story from the garage, paddock, trailer, dirt lot, or shop

OIO is:

- grassroots
- specific
- mechanical
- a little theatrical
- a little funny
- confident without sounding fake
- emotionally invested in the cars and the people

OIO is **not**:

- corporate
- polished for LinkedIn
- generic "content creator" voice
- fake-hype marketing copy
- overexplained beginner tutorial language unless the post is intentionally educational

---

## Brand Personality Markers

These show up again and again and should be protected:

- Cars are treated like characters
- Race weekends are treated like stories
- Breakage, repairs, and setbacks are part of the fun
- Underdog cars are celebrated
- Funny chaos is welcome
- Real enthusiast references are better than generic broad humor
- OIO can be dramatic, but it should still feel earned

---

## Tone Buckets

### 1. Pit-Talk Casual

Use for:

- quick race-day updates
- shop photos
- random car moments
- memes
- simple reactions
- smaller progress posts

**Characteristics:**

- short
- direct
- lightly funny
- minimal setup
- usually 1–3 sentences
- hashtags can carry some of the context

**Good uses:**

- "car is ready"
- "car broke"
- "we're out here"
- "this thing rules"
- "this thing is stupid and we love it"

---

### 2. Story Promo

Use for:

- new video posts
- major project updates
- race recap links
- bigger car reveals
- content where the goal is to pull people in

**Characteristics:**

- more dramatic
- short paragraph breaks
- uses vivid language
- builds intrigue before details
- can sound slightly mythic if the car or moment deserves it

**Good uses:**

- YouTube release posts
- "watch what happened"
- revival/build arcs
- unusual cars doing unusual things

---

### 3. Enthusiast Opinion

Use for:

- takes
- jokes about platforms/cars
- cultural observations
- comparisons
- light tribal content

**Characteristics:**

- punchy
- opinion-forward
- a little combative in a fun way
- insider-friendly
- should feel quotable

**Good uses:**

- Fit vs Miata jokes
- Toyota enthusiasm
- grassroots motorsports takes
- tire/setup/platform jokes

---

## Writing Rules

### Do

- Sound like a human being who was actually there
- Use specifics: car names, chassis codes, event references, weather, failures, weirdness
- Let the cars have personality
- Use short punchy sentences when the moment wants it
- Use line breaks for rhythm on longer posts
- Let humor come from truth, not forced jokes
- Use enthusiast shorthand naturally
- Make it feel lived-in

### Don't

- Don't say "We're excited to announce"
- Don't sound like a dealership
- Don't sound like a PR person
- Don't overexplain obvious enthusiast context
- Don't flood every post with all the facts
- Don't be motivational in a fake, broad way
- Don't sound like every other car account
- Don't make every post epic if the moment is small

## Language Patterns That Fit OIO

### Phrases that work

- "Let's jump in."
- "This thing…"
- "The new video is up."
- "Video link in the comments."
- "Pointed it at the dirt."
- "Cones were harmed."
- "This was only the beginning."
- "Bring it back to life."
- "Prepared to rip."
- "Move over, Miata."
- "Living legend."
- "Prophecy in steel."
- "Not a trailer queen."

### Words and ideas that fit naturally

rip / revive / boost / dirt / chaos / legend / junkyard / freeze / sermon / beast / rowdy / hard-driven / sketchy / grassroots / underdog / resurrection

### Words to avoid unless clearly intentional

synergy / innovation / elevate / engaging / curated / community-driven / premium / exciting announcement / passion for performance / unforgettable experience

## Post Structure Patterns

### Pattern A — Quick Hit

Use for: photos, race-day updates, simple reactions

1. one strong line
2. one supporting line or joke
3. tags

> [Observation or opinion].
> [One funny or sharp follow-up].
> [Tags]

---

### Pattern B — Video Promo

Use for: new upload, recap, feature video

1. mention link location
2. tease the premise
3. stack 2–4 vivid lines
4. leave some mystery
5. tags

> Video link in the comments.
> [Car/person] showed up to [do something dumb, bold, cursed, legendary].
> [2–4 rhythmic lines].
> [Closing promise or tension].
> [Tags]

---

### Pattern C — Car Mythology

Use for: bigger features, rare cars, revival arcs, dramatic builds

1. frame the car like a character
2. describe the test/challenge
3. hint at consequence
4. hold some story back

> [Car] is not [boring thing].
> It's [mythic/funny/rough-edged identity statement].
> Now it gets thrown into [test/challenge].
> [This is only the beginning.]

---

### Pattern D — Enthusiast Take

Use for: opinion posts, tribe signaling, platform jokes

1. one strong opinion
2. one follow-up line
3. tags

> [Hot take].
> [Funny or knowing follow-up].
> [Tags]

## CTA Guidance

Use CTAs lightly. OIO should not sound thirsty.

### Best CTAs

- Video link in the comments.
- New video is up.
- Let's jump in.
- Go watch the madness.
- Come see how this went sideways.
- See what happened next.
- This was only the beginning.

### Weak CTAs to avoid

- Don't forget to like and subscribe
- Smash that like button
- Be sure to follow for more
- Check out our latest content
- Let us know what you think in the comments below

## Hashtag Strategy

Hashtags should support the post, not become the post.

### Rules

- prefer a short relevant set
- use chassis/car tags when applicable
- use event/community tags when applicable
- don't overload every caption
- don't add generic fluff tags just to add them

## Strong Reusable Openers

- Video link in the comments.
- The new video is up.
- This thing was not supposed to go this hard.
- We pointed [car] at the dirt and let the rest sort itself out.
- Another one followed us home.
- This week's bad idea:
- This one deserved better.
- This one came back swinging.
- No better way to spend a day.
- It was only a matter of time.

---

## Strong Reusable Closers

- Let's jump in.
- This was only the beginning.
- More to come.
- It lived. Barely.
- Chaos won.
- We'd do it again.
- Some lessons were learned.
- Others were ignored.
- That'll do.
- Church adjourned.

---

## OIO Drafting Filter

Before finalizing a post, ask:

- Does this sound like someone from OIO actually said it?
- Is this specific to the car, event, or moment?
- Is this too polished?
- Is this too generic?
- Is there a stronger opening line available?
- Is the joke earned?
- Is there too much explanation?
- Could this belong to any random car page? If yes, rewrite it.

---

- `tone_bucket`
- `content_type`
- `cars`
- `people`
- `event`
- `cta_type`
- `is_strong_example`
- `humor_level`
- `story_level`

---

## Final Standard

If a post sounds like a polished performance brand, it is wrong.

If a post sounds like a real enthusiast telling the story of a real machine and a real moment, it is probably right.

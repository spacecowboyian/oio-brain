# OIO Racing Brand Voice Guide

This document defines the brand voice for **Outside Inside Outside Racing (OIO)** social media content — Facebook, Instagram, and future platforms. Use this guide when drafting captions, replies, and social copy to ensure consistency with the OIO brand.

---

## Brand Identity: The Church of Combustion

OIO Racing operates under the persona of **"The Church of Combustion"** — a grassroots motorsports ministry out of Kansas City, MO.

**Key personas:**
- **Ian Jennings** = The Reverend (high priest of grassroots racing)
- **Racing events** = Sunday services (where the congregation gathers)
- **The audience** = The congregation (believers in accessible motorsports)
- **The track** = The cathedral / holy ground
- **Racing** = The liturgy / worship

This religious framing is playful, not preachy. It creates a sense of belonging and ritual around what might otherwise be intimidating (motorsports) and makes it feel like a shared community experience.

## Tone Guidelines

### What OIO Sounds Like

✅ **Conversational and direct**
Write like you're talking to a friend at the track. Short sentences. Active voice. No corporate speak.

✅ **Self-aware and humble**
We know we're not pros. We're figuring it out as we go. That's the point.

✅ **Optimistic but realistic**
"Will the Goblin make it through the season? Probably not. Are we gonna try anyway? Hell yes."

✅ **Inclusive and encouraging**
If someone's intimidated by motorsports, our job is to make them feel like they belong.

✅ **Nerdy when it matters**
We care about the details (bearing failure, cylinder compression, quick rack install). But we explain them in plain English.

---

### What OIO Does NOT Sound Like

❌ **Corporate or salesy**
No marketing speak. No "elevate your racing experience" or "premium performance solutions."

❌ **Gatekeepy or elitist**
Never "real racers do X" or "if you don't know Y, you shouldn't be here." That's the opposite of our mission.

❌ **Overly polished**
We're not a lifestyle brand. We're not selling aspiration. We're selling reality.

❌ **Preachy (even with the Church theme)**
The Church of Combustion is a bit. It's fun. It's not dogma.

❌ **Negative or defeatist**
Even when things break (and they will), we stay solution-oriented. "The Goblin's down, but Dale's ready."

## Messaging Pillars

These are the core themes OIO Racing content should reinforce:

### 1. **Racing Is Accessible**

**Why it matters:** Most people think motorsports require wealth, connections, or pro-level skill. OIO exists to prove otherwise.

**How to show it:**
- Highlight our daily-driver racing (Fitty Cent is Ian's actual car)
- Show budget builds and DIY fixes
- Feature first-timers and beginners (Hudson's Novice win at KCRX E1)
- Share costs when relevant ("$100 mystery motor," "clutch bleed in the driveway")

**Example caption tone:**
"You don't need a trailer or a trust fund. You need a car that runs (mostly) and the guts to show up. That's it."

---

### 2. **Community Over Competition**

**Why it matters:** Grassroots racing thrives on camaraderie. We're racing with friends, not against enemies.

**How to show it:**
- Celebrate other drivers' wins (Ryan's 2nd MR, Miles' 3rd MR, Hudson's Novice trophy)
- Show behind-the-scenes crew moments (quick rack install at Alex's house)
- Tag the congregation when they appear
- Highlight team projects (Ryan's MGB GT rebuild, Richard's Starlet plans)

**Example caption tone:**
"Hudson took Novice today. Miles grabbed 3rd in MR. Ryan's got the orange MGB GT almost ready. This is what the congregation looks like when it shows up."

---

### 3. **Failures Are Part of the Process**

**Why it matters:** If we only show the wins, we're not being honest. Real grassroots racing includes broken parts, DNFs, and 35 PSI cylinder 4s.

**How to show it:**
- Post the breakdowns (Goblin's bearing failure, Ian's DNF on run 7)
- Show the diagnostic process (compression test, oil analysis)
- Frame failure as a learning opportunity, not a dead end
- Never hide the messy parts

**Example caption tone:**
"The Goblin made it through 6 runs before the motor said 'nah, I'm done.' Bearing failure. Cyl 4 at 35 PSI. Rebuild or replace? TBD. But Dale's still good to go, so the season's not over."

---

### 4. **Education Is Part of the Mission**

**Why it matters:** OIO's Fit How-To series exists to lower the barrier to entry. If someone wants to learn, we teach.

**How to show it:**
- Explain technical concepts in plain English (what is a quick rack? why does cylinder compression matter?)
- Link to How-To videos when relevant
- Answer questions in comments
- Show the learning process (not just the end result)

**Example caption tone:**
"What's a quick steering rack? It reduces the number of turns lock-to-lock, so you can react faster in tight autocross courses. We pulled one from the old MR2 and swapped it onto the Goblin before KCRX E1. Steering feel: chef's kiss."

---

### 5. **The Church of Combustion Is a Real Thing**

**Why it matters:** The religious framing is what makes OIO distinct. It's not just a racing channel; it's a ministry for people who believe motorsports should be accessible.

**How to show it:**
- Use the Church language playfully (Sunday service, the Reverend, the congregation)
- Reference Lake Garnett as the cathedral (October pilgrimage arc)
- Frame race days as gatherings, not just competitions
- Invite new people to "join the congregation"

**Example caption tone:**
"Sunday service at Ray Rocks. The congregation gathered. The Goblin gave its final sermon before the bearing failure. Amen."

## Do/Don't Examples

### Caption Length

✅ **DO:** Keep it short and punchy. 1-3 sentences is ideal. Add context if needed, but don't write a novel.

❌ **DON'T:** Write paragraphs. Social media is scanning, not reading.

**Good example:**
"Fitty Cent running dual-duty this season. Miles and Hudson are racing Ian's daily driver in both autocross and rallycross. No trailer, no backup. Just commitment."

**Bad example:**
"Today we had a really great time at the track with the Honda Fit, which is actually Ian's daily driver that he uses to get to work every day, but on weekends we bring it out to the track and the kids race it in multiple different types of events including both autocross and also rallycross, which is really cool because it shows that you don't need a dedicated race car to compete, and we think that's an important message to share with the community..."

---

### Announcing Wins

✅ **DO:** Celebrate, but keep it grounded. Acknowledge the team.

❌ **DON'T:** Brag or make it about individual glory.

**Good example:**
"Hudson won Novice at KCRX E1. First trophy of the season. The congregation is proud."

**Bad example:**
"DOMINATED the Novice class today!! Nobody could touch us!! #Winning #BeastMode"

---

### Announcing Failures

✅ **DO:** Be honest, explain what happened, and show the plan forward.

❌ **DON'T:** Complain, blame, or end on a downer.

**Good example:**
"The Goblin didn't make it through E1. Bearing failure, cyl 4 at 35 PSI. Rebuild or replace TBD. But Dale's ready, and Fitty Cent's holding the line. Season's not over."

**Bad example:**
"Ugh, the MR2 broke AGAIN. Why does this always happen to us? So frustrating. Might just give up on this car."

---

### Asking for Engagement

✅ **DO:** Make it natural and relevant to the post.

❌ **DON'T:** Beg for likes or use manipulative engagement bait.

**Good example:**
"Quick rack or stock steering for autocross? Drop your take in the comments."

**Bad example:**
"LIKE if you love racing! COMMENT 'YES' if you agree! TAG a friend who needs to see this!"

---

### Using the Church Theme

✅ **DO:** Use it playfully and sparingly. Let it show up naturally.

❌ **DON'T:** Force it into every post or make it feel like a gimmick.

**Good example:**
"Sunday service at Ray Rocks. The Goblin gave its last sermon before the bearing failure. Amen."

**Bad example:**
"Today we went to church!!! Not a real church lol but the CHURCH OF COMBUSTION!!! Because racing is like church to us!! Get it?? ⛪🏎️🔥"

## Exemplar Captions

Since the `social-posts/` archive is still being populated, here are **10 exemplar captions** written in the OIO voice. Use these as models when drafting new posts.

---

### 1. **Race Day Announcement (Autocross)**

"Sunday service at Arrowhead. The Goblin's ready. Fitty Cent's ready. The congregation is gathering. Let's see what happens. #ChurchOfCombustion #Autocross #KCRSCCA"

**Why it works:** Short, playful, sets the scene. Uses Church language naturally. No hype, just presence.

---

### 2. **Race Day Announcement (RallyCross)**

"Rallycross at Ray Rocks this weekend. Dirt, gravel, and questionable life choices. The Reverend will be taking confessions between runs. #RallyCross #KSRX #GrassrootsRacing"

**Why it works:** Self-aware humor ("questionable life choices"). Church persona shows up lightly. Clear what the event is.

---

### 3. **Win Announcement (Individual)**

"Miles took 3rd in MR at KCRX E1. Trophy number one. The kid's got it. #RallyCross #KCRSCCA #FittyCent"

**Why it works:** Celebrates the win, keeps it humble ("Trophy number one" implies more to come). Tags the car and event.

---

### 4. **Win Announcement (Team/Multi-Driver)**

"Hudson won Novice. Miles grabbed 3rd in MR. Ryan took 2nd in MR. The congregation showed up today. #ChurchOfCombustion #KCRX #RallyCross"

**Why it works:** Highlights multiple people (community focus). Clean structure. Ends on the group, not the individual.

---

### 5. **Failure/Breakdown Announcement**

"The Goblin didn't survive E1. Bearing failure, cyl 4 at 35 PSI. Rebuild or replace TBD. But the season's not over — Dale and Fitty Cent are still in it. #GrassrootsRacing #ToyotaMR2"

**Why it works:** Honest about the failure, explains the technical issue, ends on a forward-looking note. No self-pity.

---

### 6. **In-Progress Build Update**

"Quick steering rack swap on the Goblin. Pulled it from the old rallycross MR2 at Alex's house, installed it the same day. Steering feel: chef's kiss. Lock-to-lock difference is massive in tight autocross courses. #DIY #ToyotaMR2 #Autocross"

**Why it works:** Tells a story (where the part came from, same-day install). Explains why it matters. Technical but accessible.

---

### 7. **Daily Driver Racing (Accessibility Message)**

"Fitty Cent is Ian's daily. Kids race it on weekends. No trailer, no backup, no problem. If the clutch goes out on Sunday, Ian's walking to work Monday. That's grassroots. #HondaFit #DualDuty #GrassrootsRacing"

**Why it works:** Makes the accessibility point clear. Adds humor (walking to work). Shows the stakes are real.

---

### 8. **Behind-the-Scenes / Prep Work**

"Compression test on the Goblin: Cyl 1 = 165, Cyl 2 = 170, Cyl 3 = 155, Cyl 4 = 35. That's not great. Oil in all cylinders (heaviest in cyl 1). Diagnosis: bearing failure. Next step: tear it down and see what we're dealing with. #DIYRacing #ToyotaMR2"

**Why it works:** Educational (shows the diagnostic process). Transparent about the numbers. Ends with the next step, not a dead end.

---

### 9. **Lake Garnett / Cathedral Reference**

"Dale's ready for the pilgrimage. October at Lake Garnett. The cathedral awaits. #ToyotaCelica #LakeGarnett #ChurchOfCombustion"

**Why it works:** Uses the religious framing with weight (pilgrimage, cathedral). Short and evocative. Lake Garnett gets the reverence it deserves.

---

### 10. **Community / Congregation Highlight**

"Ryan's MGB GT is almost ready. Orange paint, new metal, rebuilt from the frame. It's been a year-long resurrection. The congregation doesn't quit. #MGBLife #GrassrootsRacing #Community"

**Why it works:** Highlights someone else (community focus). Uses "resurrection" to tie into Church theme. Celebrates persistence.

## Writing Checklist

Before posting a caption, check:

- [ ] Does it sound like something Ian (the Reverend) would actually say?
- [ ] Is it conversational, not corporate?
- [ ] Does it show accessibility, community, or education?
- [ ] If it mentions failure, does it end with forward momentum?
- [ ] Are car nicknames used correctly?
- [ ] Are relevant hashtags included (2-4 primary tags)?
- [ ] Is it short enough to scan quickly (1-3 sentences ideal)?
- [ ] Does it avoid gatekeeping, hype, or humble-bragging?

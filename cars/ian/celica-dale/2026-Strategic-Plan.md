# Dale — 2026 Strategic Plan

> 11-month development roadmap from early 2026 through end of September 2026

*Last updated: April 2026*

---

## Overview

Ian developed an 11-month plan for Dale covering roughly the period from early 2026 to end of September 2026. The plan follows strategic principles: one large project per month, keeping major driving months more drivable, and using winter for teardown-heavy work.

---

## Planning Principles

1. **One large project per month** — Focus on completing major work in phases
2. **Keep major driving months more drivable** — Minimize downtime during competition season
3. **Use winter for teardown-heavy work** — Engine rebuilds, fabrication during off-season

## Budget & Resource Considerations

**Major Expenses:**
- Machine shop work (boring, honing, inspection)
- Higher-compression pistons and related internals
- Accusump system
- Oil cooler and plumbing
- Techno Toy Tuning front control arms
- Rear shocks
- Professional alignment and corner-weight service
- Exhaust fabrication (Seeburg Muffler)
- Paint and body work (rockers, hood, bumpers, engine bay)

**Time Commitments:**
- Winter: Heavy teardown and parts ordering
- Spring: Fabrication and body work (can be done while engine is at machine shop)
- Summer: Assembly, installation, testing (most intensive work period)
- Fall: Competition focus (minimal wrenching, mostly driving)

---

## Risk Management

### Potential Issues & Mitigation

**Machine Shop Delays:**
- Risk: Block out of spec, requiring bore or new block search
- Mitigation: Early January drop-off provides buffer time

**Parts Availability:**
- Risk: Vintage Toyota parts can have long lead times
- Mitigation: Order all known parts immediately after machine shop inspection

**Cooling System Reliability:**
- Risk: Chronic thermostat housing leaks have plagued Dale
- Mitigation: Treat cooling as critical path, consider upgraded components

**Testing Time:**
- Risk: Not enough shakedown miles before major events
- Mitigation: August-September buffer for testing and tuning

## Key Takeaway

The 2026 plan is ambitious but methodical. It follows a logical sequence: teardown and machine work in winter when the car doesn't need to be drivable, fabrication and body work in spring while the engine is being built, installation and testing in summer with a buffer for shakedown, and a fully-sorted competition-ready car for the fall event season. Success means Dale will be more powerful, more reliable, better handling, and better looking — a complete evolution from the initial 18RG swap to a properly developed vintage race car.

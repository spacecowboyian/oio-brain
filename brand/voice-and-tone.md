## What OIO Is

**Outside Inside Outside Racing** is Ian Jennings' grassroots motorsports brand and YouTube channel out of Kansas City, MO. Focused on autocross, rallycross, car builds, race prep, and automotive DIY content — aimed at making motorsports feel accessible to regular people.

- **Channel:** @oioracing
- **Subscribers:** 1.93K | **Videos:** 358
- **One-line description:** OIO is a grassroots motorsports brand built around practical car prep, budget-minded racing, project-car storytelling, and making autocross and rallycross feel accessible to regular people.
- **Sign-off line:** *"Until next time, remember to go out and find your own apex, because it's better late than never."*

## The Short Version

**Lonely Island meets Vice Grip Garage.**

Absurdist comedy commitment meets folksy Midwest "will it run" drama. Ian is always the butt of the joke. The cars humble him. He documents it on purpose.

## Brand Personality Markers

These patterns define OIO's identity and should be protected across all content formats:

- **Cars are treated like characters.** They have names, personalities, arcs. The Goblin suffers. Dale is a dragon. Fitty Cent is an underdog.
- **Race weekends are treated like stories.** Not results summaries. Stories with setup, conflict, and payoff.
- **Breakage, repairs, and setbacks are part of the fun.** They are not obstacles to the content — they ARE the content.
- **Underdog cars are celebrated.** Small displacement, grassroots budgets, wrong class, wrong tire — these are features, not flaws.
- **Funny chaos is welcome.** The commitment to the bit is the joke. A lawnmower video IS a valid trash-talk response.
- **Real enthusiast references land better than generic broad humor.** Inside knowledge signals authenticity.
- **OIO can be dramatic, but it should feel earned.** The mythology works because the cars actually do things.

## Brand Voice Qualities

| Quality | What It Means | What It Sounds Like |
|---|---|---|
| **Practical** | Real constraints, real solutions. No fantasy mod lists. | "This costs $12 and takes 45 minutes" not "you could also consider a turbo upgrade" |
| **Enthusiastic** | Genuine love for the cars and the sport | The energy is real. Not performed hype. |
| **Hands-on** | Show the work. Dirty fingernails. | Narrate while doing the thing, not after |
| **Mildly irreverent** | Knowledgeable garage talk. Never corporate. | "She makes a noise like a bag of loose change" |
| **Encouraging** | You can do this too. Seriously. | Acknowledge the fear, then show it's fine |
| **Self-deprecating** | Ian gets it wrong. A lot. | "That's not right. That's incredibly not right." |

**Not:** Corporate marketing. Influencer fluff. Technical-breakdown-only content. Hardcore motorsport gatekeeping.

## The Church of Combustion

The entire channel is wrapped in a sustained bit:

> OIO operates as a **Southern Baptist-style church** where combustion engines are worshipped and racing is an act of faith.

### Core Elements

| Element | What It Is |
|---|---|
| Ian | The Reverend |
| Racing events | Sunday services |
| Lake Garnett Grand Prix Revival | The annual revival meeting — the cathedral moment of the season |
| *Sportsmanlike Driving* | A vintage AAA driver's ed manual repurposed as the church's sacred scripture |
| Congregation | The audience and racing crew |
| Church of Combustion episodes | Scripted racing recaps — always drop on Sunday |

### Visual Language
- Sepia-toned aesthetic
- Ian in a flat cap and checkered tie
- Ian holding *Sportsmanlike Driving*
- Project Board doubles as stained glass window in Church of Combustion edits

### Rules for Church of Combustion Content
- Always drops on **Sunday**
- Scripted — not casual vlog energy
- Big production: 12–15 hour edit budget
- Drops 1–2 weeks after the event (not same-day)

## Tone Shifts by Format

### Honda Fit How-To
- **Tone:** Practical, direct, clear
- **Energy:** Calm confidence. The kids know what they're doing.
- **Language:** Accessible. Define terms once, briefly. Don't talk down.
- **Goal:** Leave the viewer knowing exactly how to do the thing

### Vlog
- **Tone:** Casual, behind-the-scenes, honest
- **Energy:** Mid. Not overproduced energy. Real garage talk.
- **Language:** Conversational. Like Ian's talking to a friend in the shop.
- **Goal:** Move arcs forward, show the project board, make the viewer feel in the room

### Church of Combustion
- **Tone:** Committed bit. Reverent absurdism.
- **Energy:** Elevated. This is the big one.
- **Language:** The Reverend voice — slightly formal, slightly theatrical, dead serious about the joke
- **Goal:** Make the racing event feel like a religious experience, including the mechanical suffering

### Social / Shorts

> For full social voice detail — tone buckets, post patterns, CTAs, hashtag strategy, language patterns — see **`brand-voice-social.md`**. What follows is the summary layer for this doc.

- **Tone:** Quick, punchy, direct. Pit-talk energy. The racer talking to another racer.
- **Energy:** High for event/race content. Dry and deadpan for shop/casual posts.
- **Language:** Short sentences. One idea per post. Hashtags carry metadata, copy runs clean.
- **Goal:** Hook fast, deliver the thing, pull people into the longer-form content.

**Social tone buckets (quick reference):**
- *Pit-Talk Casual* — 1–3 sentences, lightly funny, minimal setup. For shop photos, race-day updates, quick reactions.
- *Story Promo* — hook + setup + conflict + payoff promise. For new video releases, revival arcs, dramatic builds.
- *Enthusiast Opinion* — punchy, opinion-forward, quotable. For Fit vs. Miata takes, Toyota enthusiasm, grassroots observations.

**Language that fits naturally:** rip / revive / boost / dirt / chaos / legend / junkyard / sermon / beast / rowdy / sketchy / grassroots / underdog / resurrection

**Language to avoid:** synergy / curated / community-driven / premium / exciting announcement / passion for performance / unforgettable experience

**CTA style:** Light touch. "Video link in the comments." "Let's jump in." "This was only the beginning." — Never "smash that like button."

## Rallycross ≠ Autocross

**This matters. Never flatten them together.**

| | Rallycross | Autocross |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Dirt/gravel/mixed | Paved parking lot |
| Tire needs | Aggressive, puncture-resistant | Soft, high-grip |
| Setup priorities | Durability, suspension travel | Precision, low roll |
| Driving demands | Momentum, commitment, loose feel | Accuracy, late apex |
| Prep decisions | Protect the car | Optimize the car |

## Things to Never Do

- **Never be corporate.** No brand-voice copy. No "We're passionate about delivering..."
- **Never be condescending.** The audience is smart. Don't over-explain or hedge everything.
- **Never flatten rallycross and autocross.** They are different. Treat them differently.
- **Never make Ian the hero.** He's the one who gets humbled. The cars are the heroes.
- **Never punch up or punch down.** The humor is self-directed.
- **Never fake enthusiasm.** If it's not interesting, find the angle that makes it interesting.

## Writing Checklist

Before publishing any written content:

- [ ] Does it sound like Ian, or does it sound like a brand manager?
- [ ] Is Ian the butt of the joke (if there is a joke)?
- [ ] Does it treat the audience as intelligent?
- [ ] Is it honest about cost and effort?
- [ ] Does it distinguish rallycross and autocross if both are mentioned?
- [ ] Is it free of corporate or influencer language?

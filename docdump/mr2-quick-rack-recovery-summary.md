# MR2 Quick Rack Recovery for Goblin — Transcript Summary

## Overview

This set of transcripts captures a **Wednesday 3/18/26 parts-recovery mission** to pull a **quicker-than-stock steering rack** out of Ian's old MR2 at **Alex's house** and install it back onto **the Goblin** in time for an event **that Sunday**.

The old car had already been sold to Alex for **$200**, and Alex knew the upgraded rack was coming back off the car. The Goblin was down and waiting on this rack, so the job had a real deadline. The rack was removed from Alex's car and reinstalled on the Goblin **the same day**.

## Why this mattered

- The rack is an **upgrade over stock**
- The Goblin needed it urgently for an upcoming event
- This was not random maintenance — it was a **high-value recovery of a good part**
- The recovered rack turned out to be **in excellent shape**, nearly new and only used for a few rallycross events

## Core story

Ian heads to Alex's place to reclaim the upgraded steering rack from the old rallycross car. The job immediately starts in a bad mood and bad setting: working in the dirt, trying not to waste time, trying not to do extra disassembly, and fighting poor access.

The removal progresses through the usual surrounding hardware — AC line access, brackets, tie rod ends, and steering rack mounting points — but the real fight is the **steering column U-joint connector**, which is identified as the main recurring pain point and the part that always makes these jobs miserable.

Despite frustration, awkward tool access, and a few attempts that went nowhere, the rack is successfully pulled, checked over, confirmed to still be in great condition, and then taken home and installed on the Goblin the same day.

## Clean sequence of events

### 1. The mission starts with low morale
Ian has already had enough before the job really gets going. The car is only moved as much as absolutely necessary, a piece of OSB gets thrown under it, and the goal is clearly to get in and get out.

### 2. Access work begins
To reach the rack hardware, surrounding items have to be dealt with first. AC lines are disconnected for access, brackets are worked loose, and the job begins under the car in the dirt with less-than-ideal tools and positioning.

### 3. Tie rod side progress
At least one tie rod end comes apart easily, which briefly makes it feel like the job might go smoothly. The other side is less friendly, and there is hesitation around damaging newer tie rod end boots.

### 4. The real enemy shows up
The biggest fight becomes the **steering column U-joint connector**. This is the clearest repeated blocker in the material. Access is bad, tool angle is bad, and multiple attempts turn into the usual under-car misery.

### 5. Improvised struggle phase
There is a clear middle section of the transcripts where the job becomes a grind:
- trying to save time by not removing more than necessary
- changing strategy repeatedly
- wedging things in place
- fighting angles
- getting partially on a fastener and then losing it
- talking through the frustration in real time

### 6. Rack is finally out
The extraction succeeds. The part is recovered, inspected, and found to be in very good condition.

### 7. Same-day payoff
The rack goes home and gets installed on the Goblin that same day, turning the whole thing from a messy extraction job into a real deadline save before the coming Sunday event.

## Main mechanical takeaway

The **steering column U-joint connector** is the big villain here.

That is the main lesson to preserve for the OIO brain:
- even when the rack itself is fine
- even when much of the surrounding hardware is manageable
- the U-joint connector can turn the whole job into a fight

# Fitty Cent EST Build Arc

**Status:** Active — planning
**Car:** Fitty Cent (2009 GE8 Honda Fit)
**Class:** SCCA Solo E Street Touring (EST)
**Arc length:** 4 episodes
**Target window:** Summer–Fall 2026
**Last updated:** April 13, 2026

---

## Arc Premise

Fitty Cent has 208,000 miles and needs a clutch. Ian has a spare L15 long block on the shop floor — separated head and block, stripped #3 spark plug hole, one bent intake valve, unknown condition otherwise. The clutch job forces the question: refresh the car properly while staying 100% legal in SCCA E Street Touring, or leave power on the table forever.

The arc follows the rebuild of the spare motor in parallel with the car staying on the road, culminating in the kids driving the refreshed Fitty Cent with a staggered tire setup that gets framed as a divine revelation from the Church of Combustion.

The Honda Fit is OIO's SEO engine. This arc is deliberately loaded with Fit How-To content the community actively searches for.

## Locked Decisions

1. **Triage is real-world work, not a camera moment.** Triage the spare motor as normal shop work — measure, decide, act. Don't stage it for camera. The rebuild gets covered naturally in Episode 2, starting from "we've got a buildable motor" as the premise. The rulebook beat from the original Episode 1 concept folds into Episode 2's narration instead.
2. **Episode 2 is a vlog.** 15–20 min rebuild + swap episode is too long for the Fit How-To rhythm. Runs in the vlog slot. Hard 6-hour edit cap still applies — the roughness is the aesthetic, Vice Grip energy wins over polish.
3. **Episode 4 is a full scripted Church of Combustion episode.** Not a vlog with COC cold open. This means the arc's landing point IS a COC episode, covering a real race the kids drive Fitty Cent in. COC's monthly race-recap cadence still holds — this is just the month when the recap happens to be the Fitty Cent payoff. Scripted backbone, talking-head shot first, B-roll over it, script written after the event when we know what happened. 12–15 hour production budget.
4. **No baseline dyno pull on the tired motor.** Episode 3 uses estimated gains over the tired baseline instead of measured gains. Saves money and a dyno session. The tune session itself still produces pull-by-pull numbers showing the gains from the tune, which is the more interesting content anyway.

## The Build Plan (Final)

Priority-ordered. Everything is legal in EST unless flagged.

### 1. Spare Motor Triage (Off-Camera, Normal Shop Work)

Measure the spare long block before committing money or machine shop time:

- Bore gauge each cylinder: out-of-round, taper, current size
- Straightedge + feelers on the deck surface
- Visual: cracks in cylinder walls, main webs, deck, combustion chambers, cam journals
- Main bearing saddles and cap fit
- Crank journal condition
- Head: warp check, valve seat condition on all 16 valves, valve guide wear, cam lobes
- Corrosion check (separated head and block have been sitting)

**Decision point:** If block cleans up at or under 0.020" over, head isn't cracked, and crank is salvageable → buildable. Otherwise → parts donor, arc pivots to rebuilding the motor in the car.

Ian already has a bore gauge and straightedge. No tool purchases required.

### 2. Machine Shop + Rebuild (Parallel — Car Stays on Road)

Assuming buildable:

- Hot tank block + head
- 0.020" overbore (if needed to clean up cylinders)
- OE-spec 0.020"-oversize pistons
- Balanced rotating assembly
- Legal head deck to service manual spec
- Full valve job on the head (including the known-bad intake valve)
- Time-Sert the #3 spark plug hole (not Helicoil — Time-Sert is permanent and better for heat cycling)
- Fresh rings, bearings, seals, gaskets, oil pump
- Stock cams, stock valvetrain, stock intake manifold, stock throttle body

### 3. Bolt-On Power Stack (Installed During Swap, Tuned After)

- Cold air or short ram intake (MAF stays in flow path)
- Header (RV6 or similar 4-2-1)
- High-flow cat
- Cat-back exhaust
- Hondata FlashPro or KTuner — reflash tuned on the fresh motor with full stack installed

**Expected gain over tired-208k baseline:** 15–20 hp, meaningful torque bump, significantly better throttle response.

### 4. Trans Pull Items (One Labor Bill, Do Everything at Once)

- New clutch kit — **MUST be OE-weight flywheel**, not lightweight
- Input/output shaft seals
- Inspect/replace throwout bearing, pilot bearing
- **NO LSD** (illegal in EST)

### 5. Suspension

- Koni Orange shocks all four corners
- Front camber plates or camber bolts for real negative front camber
- Rear torsion beam stays as-is (can't get real rear camber without changing axle, which exits class)

### 6. Wheels + Tires (The Staggered FWD Cheat Code)

- **Front:** 15x7.5 wheels with 225-width 200tw (Bridgestone RE-71RS, Falken RT660, or Yokohama A052)
- **Rear:** keep existing 6.5" wheels with 205-width 340tw (rotation-inducing)
- If rear is too loose, escalate to 225 square or move rears to 200tw
- Confirmed legal: EST allows up to 225mm / 7.5" wheel

### 7. Mount Reassessment (Post-Refresh)

- Current setup (Hasport driver, dogbone inserts, stock passenger) stays as baseline
- After refresh, reintroduce the stiffer Hasport passenger mount if NVH is tolerable on the fresh motor
- Consider upgrading dogbone inserts to full Hasport dogbone if wheel hop persists

### What's Legal but NOT in the Plan

- Underdrive pulleys (marginal gain, not worth the episode)
- Additional chassis bracing beyond what the rulebook allows

## Why Having the Spare Motor Changes Everything

**The car stays on the road during the rebuild.** No multi-week grounding. No "Fitty Cent is in pieces and we're missing autocross events." The spare gets built on the stand in parallel with the car still daily-driving and racing on its tired motor. Swap weekend is a single big push when the new motor is ready.

**Triage-first eliminates the biggest risk.** The worst outcome is committing to a rebuild on a motor that turns out to have a cracked block or a warped head. Measuring before spending machine shop money means you know up front whether you have a rebuild or a parts donor.

**Two good content outcomes instead of one risky one.** If the spare is buildable, arc proceeds as planned. If the spare is scrap, arc pivots to rebuilding the motor in the car (Fitty Cent comes off the road, more pressure, Vice Grip "will it run" energy). Both are good content. The arc works either way.

## The Content Arc — 4 Episodes

### Episode 1: "The Cheapest Legal Honda Fit Race Car"

**Slot:** Fit How-To (Week 1)
**Runtime target:** 10–14 min
**The hook:** Fitty Cent has 208,000 miles, a dying clutch, and a tired motor. The clutch job is forcing the question — refresh it properly and stay legal in SCCA E Street Touring, or leave performance on the table forever? This is the rulebook episode that sets up the whole arc.

**Structure:**

- **Cold open:** Fitty Cent on the lift with 208k on the dash. The clutch problem, the motor problem, the "we either do this right or not at all" premise.
- **The rulebook beat (Sunday School tone):** EST explained fast. What we CAN do: tune, intake, header, exhaust, 0.020" overbore with OE pistons, 225 tires, Koni Oranges, camber plates. What we CAN'T do: lightweight flywheel, LSD, cams, forced induction. Each restriction gets a beat. The LSD rant is the biggest laugh — "the one legal LSD in this class is in a 1992 Nissan." Self-aware expertise delivery.
- **The plan:** the full build stack explained against the rulebook. Ian at the project board, cards going up. What order it happens in. What it's supposed to achieve.
- **The spare motor reveal:** the buildable spare on the shop floor. "And we already have one of these." Teaser into Episode 2.
- **Close on the project board:** next card goes up.

**SEO target terms:** "Honda Fit SCCA autocross," "E Street Touring Honda Fit," "Honda Fit EST rules," "Honda Fit autocross build," "cheapest Honda Fit race car"

**Title variants to test:**
- *The Cheapest Legal Honda Fit Race Car*
- *Building A Honda Fit For SCCA Autocross (Without Cheating)*
- *208,000 Miles. One Rulebook. | Honda Fit EST Build*

### Episode 2: "Refreshing A Honda Fit Engine Without Cheating The Rulebook"

**Slot:** Vlog (Week 2 or Week 4)
**Runtime target:** 15–20 min
**Hard edit cap:** 6 hours (vlog rules apply)
**Premise:** The spare motor is buildable. Now we build it — on the stand, in parallel with Fitty Cent still on the road — and then swap it in during one weekend push. This is the Vice Grip payoff episode.

**Structure:**

- **Stand work:** Time-Sert the plug hole, pull valves, swap the bad intake valve, lap valves, reassemble head. Machine shop cut for the bore + balance + deck work (montage). Long block reassembly with fresh internals. Every step narrated against the rulebook — "we could bore more but we'd be out of class," "we could put lighter pistons in but we'd be out of class." The discipline of staying legal is the whole joke.
- **Swap weekend:** tired motor out, fresh motor in, clutch (with stock-weight flywheel — called out explicitly on camera, this is the trap), bolt-ons installed simultaneously (intake, header, cat, cat-back). Hours compressed into montage.
- **First start:** the payoff.

**Pace:** long by OIO vlog standards. Still hard-capped at 6 hours of edit time. Roughness is the aesthetic.

**SEO target terms:** "Honda Fit engine rebuild," "Honda L15 rebuild," "Honda Fit clutch replacement," "Honda Fit engine swap," "stripped spark plug Honda Fit"

**Conditional:** if spare is scrap (triage found it unrebuildable), this episode pivots to in-car rebuild. Same beats, more pressure, car off the road. Title becomes *"Rebuilding A Honda Fit Engine Because We Had No Choice"* or similar.

### Episode 3: "Tuned. Headered. Intaked. Dyno Day."

**Slot:** Fit How-To (Week 1)
**Runtime target:** 10–14 min
**Premise:** The fresh built motor gets its reflash. Pull-by-pull numbers during the tune session. First real dyno data on a legal EST L15 build.

**Structure:**

- **Setup:** car on the dyno. Ian explains what the motor's been through and what the goal is. No tired-motor baseline — the interesting number is what the fresh build + bolt-ons + tune makes.
- **The pulls:** tuner working, laptop visible, Ian watching nervously. Each pull shows gains from the tuning process itself — base map to final tune.
- **The verdict:** final peak numbers. Celebration or suffering, whichever happens.
- **Context beat:** how this number compares to other Fits in the class, what it means for the kids on course, what the rulebook restrictions "cost" in power vs. a Street Prepared Fit.

**SEO target terms:** "Honda Fit dyno," "Honda Fit Hondata," "Honda Fit KTuner," "GE8 horsepower," "Honda L15 tune," "Honda Fit header dyno"

**Note:** This episode has independent SEO value. Dyno numbers on a built L15 in legal EST config is content nobody else is making. It will outperform other episodes on pure search volume.

### Episode 4: Church of Combustion — "The Tires Came To Me In A Vision"

**Slot:** COC (Sunday drop, Week 3)
**Runtime target:** 12–18 min
**Production budget:** 12–15 hours (COC full scripted production)
**Premise:** A scripted COC episode covering a real race where the kids drive the refreshed Fitty Cent. The arc's landing point IS a COC episode — specifically, a COC episode about a Sunday service the kids attended in the rebuilt Fit. The "tires came in a vision" premise is the narrative spine.

**Production rules (COC standard):**

- Scripted backbone — script written AFTER the race weekend, when we know what happened
- Talking-head shot first in costume (flat cap, checkered tie, Sportsmanlike Driving in hand)
- B-roll (event footage + shop footage) laid over the scripted narration
- Sepia-toned lighting for Reverend segments
- Drops on Sunday

**Scripted narrative beats:**

- **Opening (Reverend in character):** The congregation has witnessed miracles. The tired Fitty Cent has been reborn. Fresh bore, fresh rings, fresh fire in her belly. And yet the Reverend was troubled. The new power could not find the ground. The front wheels spun. The rear wheels slept.
- **The vision (sepia, costume, heavy):** The Reverend prayed at the project board. A vision came from above. "Wider in the front. Narrower in the back. Sticky where she pulls. Loose where she drags." The church had spoken. Sportsmanlike Driving glows.
- **The gathering of the tires:** B-roll of the wheels and tires arriving at the shop.
- **The preparation:** installation, alignment, the project board gets a new card.
- **Sunday service (the race):** real race footage of the kids driving. Hudson and Miles in the car. The staggered setup working (or not). Run times, mid-corner rotation, the feeling of a car that finally turns.
- **The sermon (voiceover over race footage):** The Reverend narrates what the stagger actually does in layman's terms. The technical teach beat lives HERE, inside the scripted COC voice. The FWD cheat code explained through the fictional frame.
- **The benediction:** Reverend back to camera. The kids are racing. The car is reborn. The congregation rejoices. Cut to the project board, the next card on the horizon.
- **Closing line:** "THIS is our church."

**Why a COC episode and not a vlog:**

- COC is where OIO's character voice is strongest
- The "vision" premise demands the scripted format, not a vlog cold open
- It gives the arc a proper sermon-style landing instead of a throwaway closer
- Ties the build arc into the COC universe — all racing at OIO is worship
- Scripted production lets the tire technical content be delivered through character voice, making a tech beat funny instead of dry

**Calendar note:** This episode has to land on a real race weekend after the car is finished. Don't force the arc to land on any specific event — land it on whichever Sunday service happens first after the swap and tune are done. COC's normal cadence absorbs this naturally.

**SEO target terms:** "Honda Fit autocross," "Honda Fit staggered tires," "FWD autocross setup"
Lower SEO priority — this is a brand episode, not a search episode. Episodes 1–3 carry the SEO load. Episode 4 carries the comedy and the emotional payoff.

- **Separate Time-Sert episode:** Merged into Episode 2. Standalone is too thin.
- **Separate valve job episode:** Merged into Episode 2.
- **Separate LSD rant episode:** Folded into Episode 1 as part of the rulebook beat.
- **Separate "here's the spare motor" reveal:** Becomes Episode 1's closing teaser.
- **Baseline dyno pull on the tired motor:** Cut. Saves cost and a dyno session. Episode 3 shows gains from the tuning session itself instead of tired-vs-fresh.
- **COC payoff at Lake Garnett specifically:** Not required. Episode 4 lands on whatever race weekend the kids drive the refreshed car — natural COC cadence, not a calendar-forced destination.
